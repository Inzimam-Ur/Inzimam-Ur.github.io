About Me

I am a prospective PhD applicant in Economics, currently seeking opportunities for the 2026 intake. I recently completed my Master's degree in Financial Economics at Ibn Haldun University, Türkiye, where I developed a strong foundation in financial economics, econometrics, statistical analysis, and empirical research. Alongside my graduate studies, I served as a Graduate Teaching Fellow in Statistics, supporting students in developing quantitative and analytical skills.

My academic interests lie at the intersection of economics, quantitative research, and computational methods. I am particularly interested in using data-driven approaches to investigate economic and social challenges, with a growing research interest in development, education, financial economics, and social impact. I have developed practical skills in Python, R, Stata, SPSS, EViews, SQL, and MS Office, and I am continuously strengthening my knowledge of machine learning, data structures, and computational research methods.

My academic journey has been supported by competitive scholarships, including a fully funded undergraduate education and a fully funded Master's degree. Beyond formal coursework, I have actively pursued opportunities to develop my analytical and problem-solving abilities through international competitions, programming challenges, and AI hackathons. Among these experiences, I was part of the winning team at Harvard CS50x Puzzle Day 2025, where we solved all nine puzzles, and achieved a top national ranking in Advent of Code. I have also participated in international technology and AI hackathons, developing solutions to real-world problems.

Teaching, mentorship, and community engagement are equally important parts of my academic identity. Through the AKEB Bridge Camp Project, I have taught and mentored more than 3,000 students from rural areas of Pakistan over two years. I have also contributed to community development through long-term volunteer service with the Boys Scout Association and environmental initiatives with CHEPS. Internationally, as a Section Leader Mentor for Stanford University's Code in Place, I taught Python programming to more than 40 students from around the world.

My research experience has further strengthened my interest in pursuing doctoral study. During my Master's degree, I conducted research on technology and welfare economics and completed my Master's thesis using computational and data-driven techniques. These experiences have strengthened my ability to formulate research questions, work with large datasets, apply quantitative methods, and communicate empirical findings.

I am now seeking a PhD environment where I can deepen my training in economics, econometrics, and computational research while contributing to meaningful academic work. In the long term, I aspire to pursue an academic career combining high-quality research, dedicated teaching, and evidence-based engagement with economic and social challenges in developing countries.

Research With Purpose

I aim to bring together economic theory, quantitative analysis, computational tools, and a commitment to social impact to produce research that is both academically rigorous and relevant to real-world economic challenges.
