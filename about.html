About Me

I am an aspiring economist with a Master's degree in Financial Economics from Ibn Haldun University, Türkiye, and a Bachelor's degree in Economics from the International Islamic University Islamabad, Pakistan. My academic training has provided me with a strong foundation in economics, econometrics, statistics, financial economics, and empirical research. I am currently preparing for doctoral study in Economics, with the goal of developing research at the intersection of economics, quantitative methods, and computational approaches.

My research interests focus on using data-driven and computational methods to study economic and social challenges, particularly in development economics, education, inequality, public economics, financial economics, and social impact. I am especially interested in how modern computational tools, large-scale data, and machine learning can complement traditional economic and econometric methods to generate rigorous evidence for real-world economic and policy challenges.

During my Master's degree, I conducted research examining the relationship between the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the success of online crowdfunding campaigns. My Master's thesis used Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques to identify SDG-related content in a large dataset of Kickstarter campaigns and combined these measures with econometric analysis to examine their relationship with campaign outcomes. This experience strengthened my interest in empirical research, computational methods, large-scale data analysis, and the application of technology to questions in economics and social development.

Alongside my research experience, I have developed substantial teaching and mentoring experience. I served as a Graduate Teaching Fellow in Statistics at Ibn Haldun University, supporting students in quantitative and statistical methods. I also served as a Section Leader Mentor for Stanford University's Code in Place, where I taught Python programming to students from around the world. Through the AKEB Bridge Camp Project and other community initiatives, I have also contributed to the education and mentorship of students from underserved and rural communities.

My quantitative and computational skills include Python, R, Stata, SPSS, EViews, SQL, statistical analysis, and data visualization. I am particularly interested in further developing my expertise in econometrics, machine learning, natural language processing, causal inference, and computational economics during doctoral study.

Beyond formal academic work, I have actively pursued opportunities to strengthen my analytical and problem-solving abilities through international programming competitions, AI hackathons, and research projects. These experiences have allowed me to apply computational thinking to practical problems and have reinforced my interest in interdisciplinary approaches to economic research.

I am seeking a PhD environment where I can deepen my training in economics, econometrics, and computational research while contributing to meaningful scholarly work. In the long term, I aim to pursue an academic career combining rigorous empirical research, dedicated teaching, and evidence-based contributions to economic and social challenges, particularly in developing economies.

Research With Purpose

My goal is to combine economic theory, econometric analysis, computational methods, and large-scale data to produce research that advances academic knowledge while addressing meaningful economic and social questions.
